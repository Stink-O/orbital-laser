## The lasers in detail

- **Plasma.** A red designator beam and a converging reticle while it charges, then a raging plasma beam with twisting
  strands and forked lightning, a churning fireball that climbs into a mushroom cloud, a shockwave and a smoke column.
  The crater's walls fuse into obsidian, black glass and magma, lava pools on its floor crust over into obsidian, fires
  burn round the rim and rubble comes down across the land up to about 100 blocks out.
- **Godly.** A golden sigil burns into the ground while the clouds roll in and tear open overhead. A calm pillar of light
  comes down, halo rings and a pair of wings stand in the sky, six pillars of light land round it and a dome of light
  swells out. The crater turns to quartz, calcite and gold, the sigil stays etched into the ground in gold, six quartz
  pillars stand where the light came down, flowers bloom outwards in a wave, chunks of the ground are left floating above
  the crater, and players nearby are healed.
- **Black Hole.** The sky goes black and starry, a violet spiral winds in on the ground and the light round the target
  starts to bend. Then the picture flashes negative and a black hole opens 28 blocks up: light is bent round it for real
  (every view ray that passes near it is traced through its gravity), so the land and sky behind it warp into an Einstein
  ring, and an accretion disk spins up whose far side shows over the top and under the bottom, brighter on the side
  turning towards you. A jet pours out of both poles and bores down to bedrock. Blocks are torn out of the ground and
  tumble up into orbit: most spiral in, stretch, glow red-hot and vanish at the horizon, and some are slingshotted away.
  It drags the land into spiral arms of sculk and black concrete, pulls streams of rock up after it, and evaporates in a
  blinding flash that throws out what it hadn't eaten and leaves a ring of rock hanging where the disk was.
- **Meteor Storm.** 72 meteors, tiny to big, come in from high in the atmosphere trailing fire and smoke, and pound the
  land for 120 blocks round the target, scattered and in clusters, each blowing out its own crater with a shockwave, a
  meteorite at the bottom and fires round it. Then the big one comes down on the target: a white-out, a fireball that
  tears open and throws burning ejecta every way, everything above the ground flattened out to 100 blocks, a crater
  about 125 blocks across with a raised rim and a shaft to bedrock, a melt sheet of magma and lava on its floor, the
  land charred out to 135 blocks, real blocks thrown out across the land and ash coming down over everything.
- **Mothership.** A slab of dark metal over a kilometre long drops out of nowhere and stops dead over the target, its
  shadow falling across the land, the air rippling round it and its green lights cascading on. Its emitters charge and
  converge, and a green beam with a white-hot core drills a shaft to bedrock, then sweeps away from you, cutting a
  molten trench 100 blocks long, before the ship pulls away into a streak. Pick on the wheel which way it comes in
  (ahead, behind, left, right or straight down) and how it arrives (snapping into place, unfolding out of a point, or out
  of a warp streak). It reaches 1000 blocks: past your render distance it aims across Horizon's distant terrain.
- **Downfall.** The Mothership drops in as it always does and starts to charge, but the charge is cut and its power
  thrown to the batteries along its roof as a meteor the size of a hill comes in out of the sky, first a point of light
  far off, then a burning, tumbling rock. The armoured turrets turn on their rings and lay their twin barrels on it,
  recoiling as they fire, and green bolts race up and break it apart past the ship's bow; the pieces come on as fast and
  strike the hull anyway, kicking it over: fire bursts through it, chunks of it come away and fall burning, its lights
  fail and it loses lift. It lurches down as its lift gives, twice, then falls free, faster and faster, stern first,
  shedding plates, girders and burning fragments the whole way down, smoke pouring up off it, until its stern strikes,
  its bow slams down and the whole hull shudders. Blasts burst out along it, earth is flung up in sheets along both its
  sides, a shock of air races out, and it ploughs on and sinks in until it is buried half its height, crushing whatever
  stood under it and heaping the ground it shoved aside up along both sides, a wall of dust rolling out. Then the black
  hole engine in its stern fails: the stern crumples in towards it, its lights die section by section, tears open in the
  plating with a deep glow far down inside, and space starts to bend round it. It ruptures: a black hole opens in the
  wreck and tears it apart section by section from the stern, each torn piece already moving as it comes away and
  stretching as it falls in, what is left swung round and drawn in after it, the disk filling from the wreck and flaring
  as the last of it goes through, before it collapses in a flash and bores a shaft to bedrock. It uses the Mothership's
  approach and arrival choices and its own colour (red by default), reaches 1000 blocks, and always comes down well
  clear of you.
- **Planet Killer.** Out round the horizon, a kilometre off, the ground heaves and splits seven times, one after
  another, light leaking up through the cracks, and seven towers over 400 blocks tall break the surface: tiered, buttressed alien megastructures with
  glowing seams, each crowned with claws round an emitter. Each struggles
  there for a second, straining, catching, shuddering and leaning, puffs of dust venting round it, then bursts up out
  of the ground in a surge of dust rolling out over the land and a column of earth climbing with it, rock flung out,
  while the sky goes dark; each is heard grinding and bursting up from where it stands, and felt. Then each tower's
  crown crackles and a bolt of lightning feels its way out towards a ring 300 blocks over the target, 440 across, in
  steps, branching, slowly, until it suddenly runs the rest of the way and strikes it: a blinding stroke that lights the
  land and the sky, a sharp crack and a long roll of thunder. The bolts hold the jagged paths they took, trembling,
  struck again now and then, crackling where they meet the ring, the ring a band of plasma with lightning crawling over
  it, the crackle swelling as each strikes. It shudders, tightens and fills with light, and they fuse: a flash, a ring of
  light tearing out through the sky, the sky cracking open and the clouds blown away. A bolt feels its way down from the
  ring's middle with streamers reaching up off the land to meet it, and the beam pours down the path it opens, arcs
  wound round it. The land under it domes up, cracking with light; a breath of near silence, and it bursts: a fireball,
  a column of ejecta, debris spiralling up round the beam, a shock rippling out through the land and a wall of dust.
  Everything above the ground is blown flat for 340 blocks and a crater 480 across is torn out of the land from the
  middle out, its walls slumped in terraces, its floor glowing with magma, an open shaft down to bedrock under the beam;
  a broken rim of rock is thrown up round it, rays of rock streak out over the charred land, and twelve chasms split
  the land out to about 400 blocks, molten light pulsing along them. Then the bolts stutter out and the towers go dark and sink;
  the ring crushes in to a point, which drops into the crater and bursts again, and a mushroom cloud, glowing from below
  with lightning flickering in it, climbs over the crater as the daylight comes back. It reaches 1000 blocks: watch it
  from a long way off.
- **Crystal Bloom.** The land round the target wakes: a deep hum, the daylight dimming, light welling up out of the
  ground in a haze of colour and seeping through cracks that spread out over it. Where the first crystals are coming the
  ground heaves and splits, and five shards punch up out of it one after another, each breaking the ground in a burst of
  earth and rock, catching and straining there twice, then surging up a little past its height and settling; the heart,
  330 blocks tall, comes last, in the middle. Then the bloom: a wave of spires running out over the land in clusters,
  splayed and leaning, each growing in three pushes from a shard to a tower, the tallest hundreds of blocks, spraying
  earth where they break the ground. They are solid crystal, faceted: violet, magenta, rose, ice, cyan and deep blue,
  lit from inside down their hearts, flat cleavage inside them catching the light, their faces catching the sky and the
  sun, their edges glinting, and they throw coloured light over the land and the clouds. They resonate: pulses of light
  run up all of them together, faster and faster, the air shimmers round them, shards break off and orbit them, quicker
  and quicker; a breath; and they detonate in a flash of colour, every spire shattering into a storm of tumbling shards
  and a cloud of glitter, a pale shock running out over the land. About 200 fractured pieces rain down for seconds, each
  landing as blocks of its own shape, resting on the ground where it comes down; the crater is dug from the middle out, the stumps of the spires standing in it as the dig
  reaches them (the hearts of the first shards, each cluster's tallest and a few others; the rest shatter whole), and
  the fissures split open as the shock reaches them, light running along them. The well's light and a drift of motes
  fade out over the crater until the strike ends at 60 s. It reaches 1000 blocks.
- **Sinkhole.** No beam and nothing from the sky. A seismic warning; a rumble you feel more than hear, the ground
  trembling unevenly, birds going up off the land and scattering, and cracks racing out over it from the target in
  spurts, a crack heard for each, dust puffing up out of them and loose bits crumbling in: they run along the very lines
  the land will break on, hairlines at first, widening into jagged dark gaps, one side stepped down, as the slab each
  side is about to go; groans heard from out over the land. At 6 s the middle gives with a sharp
  crack: it drops four blocks, real ground and everything on it, dust puffing up, and sags three more at 7.3 s while the
  cracks round the slabs it will break into open up. At 8.6 s the collapse: a deep boom, and the ground caves in from
  the middle out, ring by ring, slowly at first, a stall while the ring round the middle holds, then in a rush. Each
  slab of land, the real land it was cut from (its blocks, the snow on it, the trees standing on it, every column at its
  own height), lurches, tips in over its outer edge and drops away into the pit, sliding in and turning as it falls, the land under it really gone; the dust boils
  up out of the hole and rolls out low over the land, rocks grind and rattle and the slabs are heard striking the
  bottom. Then an echoing near-silence, pebbles running down the walls, and the pit: about 225 blocks across, its rim
  wandering, sheer and dark down to bedrock, its walls jagged in strata bands, with ledges heaped with rubble and moss,
  roots hanging under the overhangs near the top, the world's own strata, caves and ores laid open, and a heap of
  rubble at its foot. Where it cut a river or a lake, water pours over its rim. It reaches 1000 blocks.
- **Payload.** No beam. A few seconds of calm and a grey line on the action bar (`ORBITAL TRACKING: OBJECT
  DESCENDING`); at 3 s a small, fiercely bright point shows very high in the sky (1,100 to 1,300 blocks up and 1,150
  to 1,450 out from the target, beyond it and 50 to 65 degrees round to one side, so the player sees it ahead and off to
  one side), with a bloom, a faint lens streak and a short white-hot streak of air behind it. It is shot down from
  orbit: one clean ballistic arc onto the target, a shallow glide crawling slowly over the sky for seconds, then
  steeper and faster, a thin pale trail behind it hanging in the sky in the arc's shape, drifting with the wind and
  fading; a faint high whine and a far rumble. From about 9 s it heats into a fireball, and from 10.6 s
  (`PAYLOAD INBOUND`) comes its fast final descent, steepest at the end (about 60 degrees, some 700 blocks a second),
  its head an orange-yellow fireball with a flame tail, its trail a dense dark smoke column that stays hung in the
  sky in the shape of the arc for many seconds; a roar and a tearing whistle. At 13.6 s it strikes: a brief orange flash lighting the land and the air round it and a burst of
  fire, then a column of smoke bursts up out of it, most of its height in about a second as in the clip it follows,
  eighteen billows swelling into a lumpy cauliflower mass (rounded domes on domes with creased gaps, dark slate
  blue-grey from the sky, darker down its narrower stem, its tops a little paler where the sun catches them),
  about 235 blocks tall, widening and climbing slowly on, leaning with the wind. The sound of it is late by the distance (343 blocks a second), after a near-silent breath.
  The shockwave races out over the land at the same speed as a wall of yellow-tan dust a person's height and more,
  billowing and crisp at its front, the leaves and grass near it shaken on the screen; it reaches the player as the
  blast does, shakes the camera, washes over them and settles in low drifting patches and a thin haze; dark chunks
  and specks of earth torn up across the blast's middle tumble out up to several hundred blocks and fall back, near
  the player too, the big ones trailing smoke. The column stands over the crater until the strike ends at 44 s.
  It reaches 1000 blocks.

All of them:

- **Real camera shake.** Booms and impacts shake the actual camera, timed to when the blast reaches you and weaker the
  further away you are.
- **Everything is in the world.** The effects are traced against the depth buffer and against shapes placed in the
  world (planes, cylinders, a volume), so they stay put when you move, sit behind hills in front of them and are seen in
  perspective. Nothing is painted flat on the screen but the lens: bloom, a streak and the flash.
- **Real terrain damage.** The crater, the shaft to bedrock and what each laser leaves behind are real blocks.
- **Point and fire.** Hold the designator on a target for under a second and it fires where your crosshair lands, up to
  200 blocks away (1000 for the Mothership, the Downfall, the Planet Killer, the Crystal Bloom and the Sinkhole).
- **Custom sound design.** Each laser has its own charge-up, lock-on, impact, beam, rumble and spool-down, synthesized
  from scratch, under vanilla explosions, thunder and the sound of the ground breaking.
- **Quiet.** Nothing it does shows up in chat.

## What each strike destroys

Every strike is permanent. Players are spared. The ship lasers and the Planet Killer keep the chunks they work on loaded while they work.


- **Plasma, Godly, Black Hole:** everything above the ground flattened within about 44 blocks (52 for the Black
  Hole), a crater about 60 across, a shaft to bedrock, the land scorched round it, every mob and dropped item within
  40 blocks killed. Players are spared.
- **Meteor Storm:** craters across 120 blocks round the target, then everything above the ground flattened out to
  100, a crater about 125 across, the land charred out to 135, rubble out to 250, every mob within 130 killed.
- **Mothership:** the land vaporised out to 34 blocks, a crater 22 across, a shaft to bedrock, and a trench 100 blocks
  long, up to 15 wide and 17 deep, cut away from you. The ship lasers keep the chunks they work on loaded.
- **Downfall:** a kilometre-long wreck buried at least 360 blocks in front of you, the land under its whole length
  crushed, the ground it shoved aside heaped along both sides, then the black hole's crater with arms out to 150
  blocks and a shaft to bedrock, every mob under the ship and round the hole killed. The crushing goes on for up to a
  minute after the strike, and under the far end of the hull it may be skipped past what the game has loaded.
- **Planet Killer, the biggest:** everything above the ground flattened out to 340 blocks, a crater 480 across with
  terraced walls 60 deep and an open shaft 80 across down to bedrock, a broken rim of rock out to 340, twelve chasms up
  to 40 deep out to about 400, the land charred and strewn with rubble out to 380, and every mob within 280 killed.
  Stand at least 450 blocks back. It keeps about 1,900 chunks loaded while it works; its land is done about 25
  seconds into the strike.
- **Crystal Bloom:** everything above the ground blown clear out to about 130 blocks (24 up, to the sky over its
  middle), a geode crater about 176 across and 28 deep (lined with amethyst over calcite over smooth basalt, here and
  there quartz or rose glass, seams of light through its floor, crystals jutting up off it) round a crystal-lined well 20
  across down to bedrock where the heart stood, about two dozen stumps standing up to 34 blocks tall out to about 150
  (amethyst, quartz, calcite, packed and blue ice under a skin of stained glass, banded, a glowing heart of froglight or
  sea lantern down the middle of each), three broken lengths of spire lying by their stumps, nine fissures up to 18
  deep out to about 205, crystals where the shards came down, the land frosted with calcite and amethyst out to 165 and
  strewn with amethyst out to 235, and every mob within 160 killed. Stand at least 250 blocks back. It keeps about 770
  chunks loaded while it works; its land goes in from the detonation (22 s) and is done by about 28 s.

- **Sinkhole:** a pit about 225 across (its rim wandering from about 190 to 260) down to bedrock; everything over it
  gone, every column up to its real top (a mountain it cut through, the trees and snow on it: nothing is left hanging),
  its walls cut back up to about 6 under the rim (no more than 2.5 just under the rim's own band, so the land over them
  is never left out over the pit) and their rock darkening downward (tuff, then deepslate, then blackstone), the water
  and lava in them sealed in (water turned to barrier near the top, so a lake or river stops at the edge, tuff deeper;
  lava to magma), and every mob within 140 killed. The middle is moved down 7 blocks with `clone` first. It keeps about
  285 chunks loaded while it works; its land goes in slab by slab from 8.6 s and is dug to bedrock by about 23 s.
- **Payload:** a bomb crater about 42 across (its edge wandering about 10%) and 9 deep in a bowl, its floor and walls
  scorched in broad patches (blackstone, basalt, tuff, coarse dirt, gravel, cobbled deepslate), its rim heaped up to
  about 4 high with earth and broken stone out to about 35 from the middle; everything standing on the land swept off out to
  about 85 (each column up to its real top, read with `world.read-columns`), the trees there felled: a stump of one or
  two logs, the trunk lying on the ground pointing away from the blast, its crown crushed round its end past about 46;
  the grass charred (coarse dirt, podzol, dirt) out to about 100, patchier further out; past the flattened land a band
  about 24 wide where the blast only half did its work: crowns stripped of their leaves in patches (wholly near it,
  their tops further out) and some trees knocked flat (fewer further out); about 220 pieces of rubble strewn out to
  about 105 and some 300 leaves and broken branches of the wood that grows there strewn out to about 115, landing over
  a few seconds; every mob and item within 90 killed. Nothing is dug next to water or lava, and the water and lava
  the crater would open are sealed. It keeps about 240 chunks loaded while it
  works; its land goes in at 13.65 s (`world.edit-blocks`), the rubble by about 17 s.

## Commands

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `/orbital give` | puts the Designator and the Remote in your inventory |
| `/orbital wheel` | opens the laser wheel |
| `/orbital plasma`, `/orbital godly`, `/orbital blackhole`, `/orbital meteor`, `/orbital mothership`, `/orbital downfall`, `/orbital planetkiller`, `/orbital crystalbloom`, `/orbital sinkhole`, `/orbital payload` | loads that laser |
| `/orbital approach ahead\|behind\|left\|right\|overhead` | where the ship (Mothership, Downfall) comes in from |
| `/orbital arrival snap\|unfold\|warp` | how the ship arrives |
| `/orbital terrain on`, `/orbital terrain off` | switches terrain damage (off: light and sound only) |
| `/orbital messages on`, `/orbital messages off` | switches the strikes' messages over the hotbar (replies to your own clicks still show); also the wheel's MESSAGES switch |
| `/orbital flyspeed N`, `/orbital flyspeed reset` | flies N times vanilla's creative flying speed (any number over 0, up to 1000; sprinting still doubles it), or back to vanilla's; kept between sessions and worlds; also the wheel's FLY SPEED bar and box |
| `/orbital payloadsound main`, `/orbital payloadsound alt` | which blast the Payload plays: the main one, or the alternative (its thump through the ground swaps with it); kept between sessions |
| `/orbital hud on`, `/orbital hud off` | shows or hides the status panel at the top right; also the wheel's PANEL switch, under MESSAGES |
| `/orbital skip SECONDS` | starts the next strike that many seconds in, to look at one moment of it (terrain damage off only); `0` starts at the beginning |
| `/orbital colour green\|red\|amber\|blue\|violet\|white` (or `color`) | the colour the loaded ship laser's ship is lit in, kept for each (Mothership green, Downfall red by default); also the swatches beside the arrival row |

To rebind **R**: **Options → Controls → Key Binds**, the *Orbital Laser* section at the bottom (or type "laser" in the
search box).

## The mod

The mod ([`mod/`](../mod)) is Rust built for WebAssembly, running in Horizon's mod sandbox. It is one folder: the code
(`mod.wasm`), the two items with their models and textures, and the sounds. It uses these capabilities:

| Capability | What the mod does with it |
|---|---|
| `input` | claims the two items' right-clicks (use-start, use-stop, ticks held), registers the **R** keybind, and reads what you type in the wheel's speed box |
| `ui` | draws the laser wheel: quads for the segments, item icons, text; clicks come back as events |
| `render` | the strike's post-processing pass ([`mod/shaders/strike.wgsl`](../mod/shaders/strike.wgsl)), fed 16 numbers each frame, more through `render.set-data` (the Crystal Bloom's spires and pieces, and where each piece comes to rest), and the camera shake |
| `world` | aims (a raycast up to 1024 blocks, across the distant terrain past the loaded chunks), plays the sounds, runs the crater's block changes as server commands, silently, in bulk each tick (the Planet Killer's and the Crystal Bloom's go straight into the chunks' sections through `world.edit-blocks`), asks the height of the ground under the Crystal Bloom's pieces and stumps (`world.surface-height`), reads the real blocks of the land the Sinkhole cuts into slabs (`world.read-columns`: the names of the blocks round each column's top), and sets your flying speed |
| `hud`, `storage`, `game-read` | the status panel, the chosen laser and terrain setting, the player's position and dimension |
| `registries` | the items, their textures and models, and the sounds |

The first four are new: they are on Horizon's roadmap ("planned interfaces" in its
[docs/mods.md](https://github.com/Stink-O/horizon/blob/mod-api-input-ui-render-world/docs/mods.md)), and the
[fork](https://github.com/Stink-O/horizon/tree/mod-api-input-ui-render-world) implements them so that mods built for
Horizon's API 0.1 still load unchanged. The mod reads its events with `input.poll` instead of the game calling new hooks,
which is what keeps that true. The fork's later additions (`world.set-flying-speed`, `input.poll-typed`,
`world.edit-blocks`, `world.surface-height` and `render.set-data` from the release `orbital-mod-api-5`, and
`world.read-columns` from `orbital-mod-api-6`) are needed
too: a fork build without them won't start the mod.

A strike's world changes go through a budget of 20,000 blocks and 400 commands a tick, and the rest wait for the next
tick. The Planet Killer's crater is too big for commands, so it is written straight into the chunks with
`world.edit-blocks`, a run up each column at a time, about 1.5 million blocks a second, without telling the blocks round
it (no sand falls, no water or lava flows).

## A strike

A strike is a plan built when the designator fires: every command and sound with the second it happens. The mod plays
it out tick by tick, and hands the post pass the strike's clock every frame.

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0 to 4 s | **Charge:** Plasma's red designator beam and reticle; Godly's sigil burning in and the clouds tearing open; the Black Hole's spiral and the sky going dark |
| 4 s | **Impact:** a flash, the beam comes down, a shockwave, everything standing within about 44 blocks is vaporised |
| 4 to 13 s | **Burn:** the beam, the fireball or the dome of light, the crater dug layer by layer and the shaft drilled to bedrock |
| 13 to 26 s | **Aftermath:** what each laser leaves behind lands, the light fades, the smoke or the light veil disperses |

The Meteor Storm runs longer: the storm from 4 to about 16 s, the big one coming in from about 11 s and hitting at
17.5 s, then its crater, ejecta and ash until 38 s. The Mothership arrives at 3 s, charges until 8, drills from 8.2 s,
sweeps from 10 to 19 s, cuts off at 20 and is gone by 23.5; its trench cools until 34 s. The Downfall arrives at 3 s
and charges from 4 (its meteor is a point of light far off from 2.5 s); the charge is cut at 6.8, its batteries fire
from 8.4, the meteor breaks up at 11 and its pieces strike at about 15.4; it lurches down at 17 and 20, falls free,
strikes at 30, its bow slams down at 30.65 and it is still by 32.6; its engine groans from 34, ruptures at 40, the last of
the wreck goes in at about 53, and the black hole collapses at 55; the strike ends at 64. The Planet Killer's towers'
ground heaves from 0.1 s, they break the surface from 0.8 s, struggle there for 1.1 s and burst up 0.8 s later, one
every 0.4 s; their bolts set out from 3.05 to 5.45 s and strike the ring 1.14 s after they set out, the last by about
6.6; it shudders and tightens from 6.9 and they fuse at 7.6 s; a bolt feels its way down and the beam pours down it at
8.4, lands at 8.5, and the land bursts at 9; the chasms open over the next 2 s, the crater is dug from the middle out
from 11 s and the land is done by about 25 s; the towers cut out at 17.65, the ring crushes in, drops into the crater
and bursts at 18.55, and the mushroom climbs until the strike ends at 30.5. The Crystal Bloom's land wakes from the
start; its first shard breaks the ground at 3.5 s and the next every 0.62 s (each heaving 0.7 s before, catching 0.34
and 0.66 s after it breaks through and surging up 0.9 s after), the heart at 6.7; the bloom sets out from the middle at
7.6 and reaches the edge of the field 4.6 s later; they resonate from 14, shards orbiting them from 15.6, a breath from
21.68, and detonate at 22; the crater is dug from the middle out from 22.1 at 70 blocks a second, the shards land
until about 28, the broken spires come down at 24.6 and the land is frosted and strewn by 28.2; the well's light and
a drift of motes fade out over the crater until the strike ends at 60. The Sinkhole's cracks race out from about 1 s,
the birds go up from 1 s, the middle gives at 6 and sags at 7.3; the collapse sets out from the middle at 8.6, reaches
30 blocks by 9.5, holds until 10.6, then rushes out to the rim by about 15 (each slab drawn in place of its land from
when that is dug, 0.1 s before it lets go, and gone into the dark 3.2 s after); the pit is dug on down to bedrock from
16 s, a chunk column at a time from the middle out, done by about 23; the dust settles by 34, the pit's dark fades out
slowly from 31 and the strike ends at 40. The Payload is calm until 3 s, comes in high across the sky until 10.6, comes down fast and strikes at 13.6;
its blast, the dust's front and the jolt reach the player at 13.6 plus their distance over 343; the column rises
until about 20 s and goes on growing slowly, the debris is down by about 29, and everything fades out from 37 to 44.

## The effects

The effects are one fragment shader, run as the mod's post pass after the fork's small prelude (the world's colour and depth, the camera and the mod's parameters). It rebuilds every pixel's position from
the depth buffer, so ground effects (the reticle, the sigil, the crater glow, the shockwaves) sit on real blocks. The beams
and pillars are cylinders found by the closest approach between the view ray and their axis, the halo rings and wings are
planes in the world, Plasma's fireball is a noise volume traced along the view ray, and Godly's opening in the clouds is
a plane far overhead. The Black Hole marches each view ray that passes near it through its gravity (the photon orbit
equation), picking up the accretion disk where the ray crosses it and resampling the scene in the direction the ray
leaves in; the debris round it are ray-traced boxes on paths the mod's block changes follow too. The Meteor Storm's meteors are
placed by the same hash in the shader and in the mod, so each crater opens where its meteor lands; their trails, the
fireball and the smoke are noise volumes traced along the view ray. The Mothership's hull is found analytically: each
view ray meets its stepped decks as flat floors and its walls by testing the outline, so it is cheap however much of the
sky it covers; its arrival and departure are averaged over a shutter for real motion blur. The Downfall has a pass of its
own ([`mod/shaders/downfall.wgsl`](../mod/shaders/downfall.wgsl), after the helpers in `strike.wgsl`), so its size costs the
other lasers nothing: the same hull, met in its own frame as it tilts (from any side, roof and batteries too), the
Black Hole's lens, and its dust as a volume integrated along the view ray in closed form. Its fall, crash, gouge,
chunks and the blocks the black hole tears up are worked out by the same formulas in the shader and in the mod. The
Planet Killer has its own pass too ([`mod/shaders/planetkiller.wgsl`](../mod/shaders/planetkiller.wgsl)): its towers are
solids marched in their own frame (a signed distance: plinth, buttresses, tiers, collars, crown; sheared as they lean),
the nearest hiding what is behind it, its bolts are jagged lines from a seeded hash, met kink by kink by the
view ray (their broad bends first, the kinks only near them), so each keeps the path it took from frame to frame, the
ring is met where the view ray crosses its plane (a clean ellipse from any side), its heart, dome and fireballs are
traced as spheres and domes, the towers' dust and the mushroom cloud are noise volumes marched along the view ray, and
the dust wall is a cylinder; its towers and its chasms are placed by the same hash in the shader and in the mod, and the
crater glows where it has just been opened as the mod digs it from the middle out. The Crystal Bloom has its own pass
too ([`mod/shaders/crystalbloom.wgsl`](../mod/shaders/crystalbloom.wgsl)): each spire is a convex crystal (a hexagonal
prism drawing in up its length under a six-faced tip, its faces a little uneven), so the view ray is met exactly, plane
by plane, and the face it enters by is a real facet; inside it the ray is bent in (each colour a little differently),
followed to the face it leaves by and out again, past the cleavage sheets inside, through the crystal's body, and the
nearest spire hides what is behind it. The shards are octahedra met the same way, the glitter is points of light on
shells round the burst found where the view ray crosses each, and the dust is a noise volume. The spires, their breaks,
the pieces' cuts, throws and spins and the fissures are worked out once by the mod as the strike starts (`cb_table`)
and handed to the pass with `render.set-data`, so the pass reads them rather than hashing them again for every pixel
(about half its GPU time at the heaviest moments). Each stump is built where its spire stood, each piece is built as
blocks where it lands, and the crater glows only where the mod has really dug it. The pass only knows the land at the target's height, so the mod asks the world how high the ground
really is under each of the ~200 pieces that fly out (`world.surface-height`, a pair a tick from 13 s) and rests each one
whole on it: on a low point of the ground under it, so it is wedged in rather than hovering, deeper into the high side
of a slope or a cliff where it would otherwise hang over the ground. It gives the pass how far up or down that is
(`render.set-data`), long before the burst; the pass throws the piece that much higher over its flight, so the last pose
it draws is exactly where the blocks are built, block for block the same piece. The stumps are built where they stand,
down to the ground wherever it lies lower, and the crystals round their feet rest on the ground at their feet.

## The sounds

The sounds ([`tools/synth_sounds.py`](../tools/synth_sounds.py)) have no samples in them: they are generated from noise
and sweeps, with a reverb built from a synthetic impulse response. The Planet Killer's bursts
([`tools/sounds_pk_blast.py`](../tools/sounds_pk_blast.py)) are shaped to measurements of a real, very large explosion:
its spectrum, how it rises and holds, how its bands move and how wide it sounds over time. Only the numbers were taken
from the recording; none of its audio is used. The Crystal Bloom's ([`tools/sounds_crystal.py`](../tools/sounds_crystal.py))
are built from the modes of struck glass (inharmonic partials, each a pair of sines a hair apart so it shimmers), glints
of a few milliseconds by the thousand, and bursts of filtered noise for glass breaking; its detonation is the Planet
Killer's `explosion_live` on its own seed and with its own character, with ringing crystal, glitter and shattering glass
on top of it. The Sinkhole's ([`tools/sounds_sinkhole.py`](../tools/sounds_sinkhole.py)) are rock and earth: a sub-bass
rumble that swells and sags unevenly, groans of the ground under strain (narrow bands of noise sliding down, rock
grinding in uneven grabs), sharp cracks of rock each with its own splinters and a crumble of grit, the give's drop, the
collapse (`explosion_live` again, on its own seed, made earthy: its top rolled off, its low-mid pushed, a strain before
it, rock grinding as it gives), the rockfall (a grinding roar and slabs striking the bottom, unevenly, further and
further off) and the pebbles after, each bouncing its own way, in the pit's long echo. Each is heard late by how far
away it is. The Payload's ([`tools/sounds_payload.py`](../tools/sounds_payload.py)): a faint thin whine high up, a far rumble under it; the dive's whistle bending up and a roar of air and fire tearing louder, cut
dead at the strike; the ground's thump and rumble arriving first, through the land at five times the air's speed
(`ground`), with a faint early jolt; a near-silent breath while the blast travels; the blast (`explosion_live` on its
own seed, as loud as the Planet Killer's); the dust's rush and the wind gusting on after it; clods thudding down round the player and a
patter of grit; and a long tail of rolls off the land, further and further off. `/orbital payloadsound alt` swaps the blast and the
thump for alternatives in the manner of a sound designer's redesign (`blast_alt`, `ground_alt`); `main` swaps them back.

The Sinkhole's pass ([`mod/shaders/sinkhole.wgsl`](../mod/shaders/sinkhole.wgsl)) reads the rings and slabs the mod
cuts the land into (`sh_table`, through `render.set-data` on the tick after it fires), each slab's ground height and
slope once the world has said them (`world.surface-height`), the waterfalls, where the rim's columns stand under
water, and the land itself. As it fires the mod reads the 24 blocks under the top of every column of the pit
(`world.read-columns`, six queries of ~8,000 columns) and works them out ~3,000 columns a tick
([`mod/src/strike/sh_land.rs`](../mod/src/strike/sh_land.rs)) into three words a column: its ground's height, the block
on top, the one under it and one deeper (by name: about 60 materials, each a top and side colour from the game's own
textures and how much it varies block to block; grass and most leaves tinted by the biome's grass and foliage colours
the world answers with, the poplars', cherry's, azalea's, pale oak's, spruce's and birch's leaves their own colours),
snow lying on it, a trunk and the leaves over it, the slab it is part of, and whether water stands on it (a slab
carries the bed, and no crack crosses water); plus each slab's bounds. Columns not loaded yet are asked again, and a
slab is drawn as its land only once all of it is in.
The pass draws each slab as those blocks, marching the view ray through its columns cell by cell (a 3D DDA inside its
bounds, after a bounding sphere), each face shaded as the game shades it and textured with texels that fade out once
smaller than a pixel; it draws it in its place from the moment the mod digs the land under it (the same blocks where
the real ones were), then tips and drops it, its trees going with it, lit as the land round it (the sky's light for the time of day) until
it sinks below where its ground was, then into the pit's shadow and dark by how deep it is. A slab whose land was not read is a block of
grass over dirt over stone, as before. The dig clears each column up to that real top. The cracks are the slabs' own
edges, reached by spurts racing out from the middle (`sh_spurts` in the mod, `sh_crack_at` in the pass), drawn only on
the ground's top (the land's heights), their width against the pixel's footprint across them from the derivatives of
the ground's position, never thinner on screen than most of a pixel (wider and fainter instead) and faded out far off,
the column under a pixel looked up a little inside the block it is on, so nothing flickers; the waterfalls pour from
the water's own surface over the rim as it wanders, over a lip and faster and faster a little out from the wall, in
strands of the water's biome tint with darker gaps, white where they break up, mist into the dark;
the pit is darkened where it has really been dug, the dark fading out over the strike's last 9 s; the dust is a noise
volume marched along the view ray.


The Payload's pass ([`mod/shaders/payload.wgsl`](../mod/shaders/payload.wgsl)) reads the object's path, sampled 128
times from 3 to 13.6 s, and the column's eighteen billows (`pl_table`, through `render.set-data` on the tick after it
fires; the player's position as it fired is in its params). The trail is drawn through the path's samples as soft
tubes of smoke (each bit of it carried by the wind and spreading as it ages, broken up by noise, lit on its sunward
side); the column is marched through the billows' smooth union (worked out once a pixel), its surface bulged in
cellular domes at two scales (rounded tops, creased gaps), each lump lit by a look a few blocks towards the sun and
the column's bulk shadowing it with two more, lit by the sky the scene shows; the dust is marched only through the
shell just behind the shockwave's front (and sparsely inside it), over the land's own ground: a 96 x 96 heightfield
of it asked of the world as it fires (`world.surface-height`, from about 260 to 1,150 blocks round the target as the
player is near or far), sent from `PL_HF` with how sheltered each cell is from the blast by the land between, and close
to where each pixel's ray meets the land, that point's own height, so the dust climbs the rises, fills the hollows and
is thinner and later in the lee of a ridge; it stops at the column. As the front washes over the eye (within about 40
blocks of the ground), the picture is greyed and flattened by the dust, clearing over a few seconds, and over the second
after it muck lands on the lens, out of focus, as measured from a reference clip's dirt: drops near round but each its
own (a few stretched), 2 to 7% of the picture's height, a ring darker than the middle, soft at the edge, of very
different strengths, often with a smaller drop beside them, nearly all high on the screen, fading over ten seconds or so; the wall is thinned right round the eye so the picture over it stays clear; the debris is 120 ballistic chunks with drag, torn up across the blast's middle, each blurred
along its motion, tumbling, the big ones trailing smoke. It costs about 0.3 to 2.4 ms at the preview's 800x450. The jolt as the front reaches the player is
`render.shake` worked to match the clips: a hard hit, sharp aftershocks every other tick for 0.7 s, then a shudder of
smaller quick jolts fading over the next 1.7 s, over a long faint rumble.
`tools/preview.py payload ... --views plref` looks at it as the clip it follows was filmed (450 blocks off in a wood,
a 59 degree field of view); `plhill`, `plhilllow` and `plhillside` over hills.
## Stock Horizon

0.1's setup ran on stock Horizon builds from before 24 October 2026, when Horizon removed shader packs (it won't work on newer ones), with Plasma and Godly (the other lasers are mod-only): a shader pack, a sound pack and the item mod, run by
[`strike.py`](../strike.py) from outside the game through Horizon's `horizon-ctl`. The items are spyglasses there, the remote
opens a chest-style menu to pick the laser, and every strike rewrites the shader pack's settings.

```sh
python3 install.py --legacy --enable    # with the game closed
horizon-ctl launch --world MyWorld --creative --wait
python3 strike.py --doctor              # says what's missing and how to fix it
python3 strike.py --daemon              # hold the designator (slot 9) to fire; the remote (slot 8) picks the laser
```
