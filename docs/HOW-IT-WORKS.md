## The lasers in detail

- **Plasma.** A red designator beam and a converging reticle while it charges, then a raging plasma beam with twisting
  strands and forked lightning, a churning fireball that climbs into a mushroom cloud, a shockwave and a smoke column.
  The crater's walls fuse into obsidian, black glass and magma, lava pools on its floor crust over into obsidian, fires
  burn round the rim and rubble comes down across the land up to about 100 blocks out.
- **Godly.** A golden sigil burns into the ground while the clouds roll in and tear open overhead. A calm pillar of light
  comes down, halo rings and a pair of wings stand in the sky, six pillars of light land round it and a dome of light
  swells out. The crater turns to quartz, calcite and gold, the sigil stays etched into the ground in gold, six quartz
  pillars stand where the light came down, flowers bloom outwards in a wave, chunks of the ground are left floating above
  the crater, and players nearby are healed.
- **Black Hole.** The sky goes black and starry, a violet spiral winds in on the ground and the light round the target
  starts to bend. Then the picture flashes negative and a black hole opens 28 blocks up: light is bent round it for real
  (every view ray that passes near it is traced through its gravity), so the land and sky behind it warp into an Einstein
  ring, and an accretion disk spins up whose far side shows over the top and under the bottom, brighter on the side
  turning towards you. A jet pours out of both poles and bores down to bedrock. Blocks are torn out of the ground and
  tumble up into orbit: most spiral in, stretch, glow red-hot and vanish at the horizon, and some are slingshotted away.
  It drags the land into spiral arms of sculk and black concrete, pulls streams of rock up after it, and evaporates in a
  blinding flash that throws out what it hadn't eaten and leaves a ring of rock hanging where the disk was.
- **Meteor Storm.** 72 meteors, tiny to big, come in from high in the atmosphere trailing fire and smoke, and pound the
  land for 120 blocks round the target, scattered and in clusters, each blowing out its own crater with a shockwave, a
  meteorite at the bottom and fires round it. Then the big one comes down on the target: a white-out, a fireball that
  tears open and throws burning ejecta every way, everything above the ground flattened out to 100 blocks, a crater
  about 125 blocks across with a raised rim and a shaft to bedrock, a melt sheet of magma and lava on its floor, the
  land charred out to 135 blocks, real blocks thrown out across the land and ash coming down over everything.
- **Mothership.** A slab of dark metal over a kilometre long drops out of nowhere and stops dead over the target, its
  shadow falling across the land, the air rippling round it and its green lights cascading on. Its emitters charge and
  converge, and a green beam with a white-hot core drills a shaft to bedrock, then sweeps away from you, cutting a
  molten trench 100 blocks long, before the ship pulls away into a streak. Pick on the wheel which way it comes in
  (ahead, behind, left, right or straight down) and how it arrives (snapping into place, unfolding out of a point, or out
  of a warp streak). It reaches 1000 blocks: past your render distance it aims across Horizon's distant terrain.
- **Downfall.** The Mothership drops in as it always does and starts to charge, but the charge is cut and its power
  thrown to the batteries along its roof as a meteor the size of a hill comes in out of the sky, first a point of light
  far off, then a burning, tumbling rock. The armoured turrets turn on their rings and lay their twin barrels on it,
  recoiling as they fire, and green bolts race up and break it apart past the ship's bow; the pieces come on as fast and
  strike the hull anyway, kicking it over: fire bursts through it, chunks of it come away and fall burning, its lights
  fail and it loses lift. It lurches down as its lift gives, twice, then falls free, faster and faster, stern first,
  shedding plates, girders and burning fragments the whole way down, smoke pouring up off it, until its stern strikes,
  its bow slams down and the whole hull shudders. Blasts burst out along it, earth is flung up in sheets along both its
  sides, a shock of air races out, and it ploughs on and sinks in until it is buried half its height, crushing whatever
  stood under it and heaping the ground it shoved aside up along both sides, a wall of dust rolling out. Then the black
  hole engine in its stern fails: the stern crumples in towards it, its lights die section by section, tears open in the
  plating with a deep glow far down inside, and space starts to bend round it. It ruptures: a black hole opens in the
  wreck and tears it apart section by section from the stern, each torn piece already moving as it comes away and
  stretching as it falls in, what is left swung round and drawn in after it, the disk filling from the wreck and flaring
  as the last of it goes through, before it collapses in a flash and bores a shaft to bedrock. It uses the Mothership's
  approach and arrival choices and its own colour (red by default), reaches 1000 blocks, and always comes down well
  clear of you.
- **Planet Killer.** Out round the horizon, a kilometre off, the ground heaves and splits seven times, one after
  another, light leaking up through the cracks, and seven towers over 400 blocks tall break the surface: tiered, buttressed alien megastructures with
  glowing seams, each crowned with claws round an emitter. Each struggles
  there for a second, straining, catching, shuddering and leaning, puffs of dust venting round it, then bursts up out
  of the ground in a surge of dust rolling out over the land and a column of earth climbing with it, rock flung out,
  while the sky goes dark; each is heard grinding and bursting up from where it stands, and felt. Then each tower's
  crown crackles and a bolt of lightning feels its way out towards a ring 300 blocks over the target, 440 across, in
  steps, branching, slowly, until it suddenly runs the rest of the way and strikes it: a blinding stroke that lights the
  land and the sky, a sharp crack and a long roll of thunder. The bolts hold the jagged paths they took, trembling,
  struck again now and then, crackling where they meet the ring, the ring a band of plasma with lightning crawling over
  it, the crackle swelling as each strikes. It shudders, tightens and fills with light, and they fuse: a flash, a ring of
  light tearing out through the sky, the sky cracking open and the clouds blown away. A bolt feels its way down from the
  ring's middle with streamers reaching up off the land to meet it, and the beam pours down the path it opens, arcs
  wound round it. The land under it domes up, cracking with light; a breath of near silence, and it bursts: a fireball,
  a column of ejecta, debris spiralling up round the beam, a shock rippling out through the land and a wall of dust.
  Everything above the ground is blown flat for 340 blocks and a crater 480 across is torn out of the land from the
  middle out, its walls slumped in terraces, its floor glowing with magma, an open shaft down to bedrock under the beam;
  a broken rim of rock is thrown up round it, rays of rock streak out over the charred land, and twelve chasms split
  the land out to about 400 blocks, molten light pulsing along them. Then the bolts stutter out and the towers go dark and sink;
  the ring crushes in to a point, which drops into the crater and bursts again, and a mushroom cloud, glowing from below
  with lightning flickering in it, climbs over the crater as the daylight comes back. It reaches 1000 blocks: watch it
  from a long way off.
- **Crystal Bloom.** The land round the target wakes: a deep hum, the daylight dimming, light welling up out of the
  ground in a haze of colour and seeping through cracks that spread out over it. Where the first crystals are coming the
  ground heaves and splits, and five shards punch up out of it one after another, each breaking the ground in a burst of
  earth and rock, catching and straining there twice, then surging up a little past its height and settling; the heart,
  330 blocks tall, comes last, in the middle. Then the bloom: a wave of spires running out over the land in clusters,
  splayed and leaning, each growing in three pushes from a shard to a tower, the tallest hundreds of blocks, spraying
  earth where they break the ground. They are solid crystal, faceted: violet, magenta, rose, ice, cyan and deep blue,
  lit from inside down their hearts, flat cleavage inside them catching the light, their faces catching the sky and the
  sun, their edges glinting, and they throw coloured light over the land and the clouds. They resonate: pulses of light
  run up all of them together, faster and faster, the air shimmers round them, shards break off and orbit them, quicker
  and quicker; a breath; and they detonate in a flash of colour, every spire shattering into a storm of tumbling shards
  and a cloud of glitter, a pale shock running out over the land. About 200 fractured pieces rain down for seconds, each
  landing as blocks of its own shape, resting on the ground where it comes down; the crater is dug from the middle out, the stumps of the spires standing in it as the dig
  reaches them (the hearts of the first shards, each cluster's tallest and a few others; the rest shatter whole), and
  the fissures split open as the shock reaches them, light running along them. The well's light and a drift of motes
  fade out over the crater until the strike ends at 60 s. It reaches 1000 blocks.
- **Sinkhole.** No beam and nothing from the sky. A seismic warning; a rumble you feel more than hear, the ground
  trembling unevenly, birds going up off the land and scattering, and cracks racing out over it from the target in
  spurts, a crack heard for each, dust puffing up out of them and loose bits crumbling in: they run along the very lines
  the land will break on, hairlines at first, widening into jagged dark gaps, one side stepped down, as the slab each
  side is about to go; groans heard from out over the land. At 6 s the middle gives with a sharp
  crack: it drops four blocks, real ground and everything on it, dust puffing up, and sags three more at 7.3 s while the
  cracks round the slabs it will break into open up. At 8.6 s the collapse: a deep boom, and the ground caves in from
  the middle out, ring by ring, slowly at first, a stall while the ring round the middle holds, then in a rush. Each
  slab of land, the real land it was cut from (its blocks, the snow on it, the trees standing on it, every column at its
  own height), lurches, tips in over its outer edge and drops away into the pit, sliding in and turning as it falls, the land under it really gone; the dust boils
  up out of the hole and rolls out low over the land, rocks grind and rattle and the slabs are heard striking the
  bottom. Then an echoing near-silence, pebbles running down the walls, and the pit: about 225 blocks across, its rim
  wandering, sheer and dark down to bedrock, its walls jagged in strata bands, with ledges heaped with rubble and moss,
  roots hanging under the overhangs near the top, the world's own strata, caves and ores laid open, and a heap of
  rubble at its foot. Where it cut a river or a lake, water pours over its rim. It reaches 1000 blocks.
- **Payload.** No beam. A few seconds of calm; at 3 s a small, fiercely bright point shows very high in the sky (1,100 to 1,300 blocks up and 1,150
  to 1,450 out from the target, beyond it and 50 to 65 degrees round to one side, so the player sees it ahead and off to
  one side), with a bloom, a faint lens streak and a short white-hot streak of air behind it. It is shot down from
  orbit: one clean ballistic arc onto the target, a shallow glide crawling slowly over the sky for seconds, then
  steeper and faster. As it strikes the thick air there is a double crack on a sub boom, then it crackles in distinct,
  uneven pops high in the sky (a few a second, thickening, dulled and smeared by the air, the bigger ones rumbling, each
  coming back faintly off the land), over a faint high whine and a far rumble, all of it one sound high overhead, dulled by
  the air, until it opens out round the player in its last second. Its trail is a cord of lumpy
  grey-brown smoke (a thin pale vapour for its first second or two) hung in the sky in the arc's shape: dense where it
  is laid, then swelling, paling, breaking into beads and puffs, fraying and thinning away over twenty seconds or so,
  drifting with the wind. From about 7 s it sheds pieces: eight glowing chunks flung off to the side, falling away
  behind it and burning out over a second or three, each trailing its own thin smoke, and in the last seconds a spray
  of sparks off the fireball. From about 9 s it heats into a fireball, and from 10.6 s comes its
  fast final descent, steepest at the end (about 60 degrees, some 700 blocks a second), its head an orange-yellow
  fireball with a soft teardrop of flame behind it, the smoke just behind that lit orange; a roar, a tearing whistle
  and the pops coming thicker and bigger. At 13.6 s it strikes: a brief orange flash lighting the land and the air round it and a burst of
  fire, then a column of smoke bursts up out of it, most of its height in about a second as in the clip it follows,
  eighteen billows and a ragged trunk under them melting into one lumpy cauliflower mass (big lumps broken by middling
  and small ones, its edge crisp in places and a torn, wispy fringe in others; tan smoke and dust, browner at its
  dusty foot, lit by the scene: sunlit tops, greyed by the sky on its shadowed side, darker deep in it and down its
  narrower stem; its foot sunk under the crater's floor in a low, wide skirt of dust, so it rises out of the ground; lit
  by the sun as the hour has it, warm orange-red as it nears the horizon, its sunward side lit and the far side dark,
  the moon's faint cold light at night), about 235 blocks tall, widening and climbing slowly on, leaning with the wind from its foot up (the foot stays planted over the crater; the head drifts furthest). The sound of it is late by the distance (343 blocks a second), after a near-silent breath.
  The shockwave races out over the land at the same speed as a wall of yellow-tan dust a person's height and more,
  billowing and crisp at its front, the leaves and grass near it shaken on the screen; it reaches the player as the
  blast does, shakes the camera, washes over them and settles in low drifting patches and a thin haze; dark chunks
  and specks of earth torn up across the blast's middle tumble out up to several hundred blocks and fall back, near
  the player too, the big ones trailing smoke. The column keeps its crisp billows for about 20 s; then, as in the
  clip it follows, it loses them: from 35 s to about 48 s the billows melt into a rounded, mottled mass with a broad
  soft fringe, its stem lifting off the ground and thinning first, the whole drifting, widening and evenly lit, and
  it thins to nothing by about 65 s. The strike ends at 70 s.
  It reaches 1000 blocks.
- **Nuke** (page three). No beam. From 0.8 s the bomb comes in from orbit far beyond the target and off to one side: a white-hot streak across the
  sky, its plasma sheath and glowing wake behind it, a thick lumpy trail of ablated smoke and burning pieces torn off
  it. It sheds its heat by about 7 s, its casing (an ogive nose, four fins) showing, the sun glinting off it, and
  arcs over into a steep dive, faster and faster (to about 330 blocks a second), spinning up so the glints come
  quicker, its vapour trail thickening, a collar of vapour round it and its nose glowing again in the last seconds (a
  strobe on it at night), a pinprick of white at it the instant before it bursts. Its fall whistles down from high overhead. At 11 s it bursts 12 blocks over the land (the ground round the target, not a treetop it hit), in
  silence: the picture whites out for a moment, the land and the sky lit white then orange, and a white-hot fireball
  swells to about 100 across within a second. It cools through yellow and orange to a dull red glow inside the cloud
  as it rises, rolling into a mushroom cloud: a churning toroidal cap with a rounded crown, lit from inside and from
  below at first, about 400 blocks up by 30 s, on a stem pulled up out of the land with its lumps boiling up it, a
  wispy white condensation ring round the stem from 15 to 40 s, a skirt of dust round its foot, and its shadow thrown
  across the land. The shockwave races out at 343 blocks a second, a shell bending the light at its edge, raising a
  low wall of grey-brown dust; when it reaches the player the boom arrives with it (after the ground's thump, which
  comes first through the land), the camera is jolted hard (the picture itself knocked about on top of the game's
  shake) and shudders for eight seconds or so, the player is knocked back and up as by an explosion (thrown a little
  over a jump's height and some six blocks back within 200 blocks, less further off, none past 600), and muck lands on the lens, out of focus. Earth and debris rain
  down for half a minute. From 59 s the cloud loses its billows into a drifting haze, gone by 90 s, when the strike
  ends. It reaches 1000 blocks.
- **Rod from God** (page three). No beam, no explosive: a colossal rod of dark forged metal dropped from orbit, and
  holy. From 1 s a golden-white star some 4,100 blocks up, plumb over the target,
  comes straight down, faster and faster (250 to about 1,000 blocks a second a second before it strikes), then over its
  last half second a hard last burst, eased in, to about 2,300, so it slams in while it still reads frame by frame; god rays stream out of it, broken by whatever stands between, a column of
  light runs down its path from the sky to the target with the ionised line it lays in it (each bit of the line
  widening and dimming as it ages, the oldest first), a starburst, a streak and ghosts in the lens, the air shimmering
  round it and its light lying on the land under it; for its last moments its shape: a rod 13 blocks thick and 171
  long, nose down, its nose white-hot, gold and carved bands round it, a bow of glowing air round its nose and in its
  last second the shock cone. At 7 s it strikes: impact frames (the picture held on the hit for a fifth of a second while hard cuts of two or three frames play over it, by default Kanada style: black silhouettes with thick lines converging on the point, on white; a close-up three times as near in negative with gold lines; gold and umber two-tone; each cut punching the camera in and shaking it; then a zoom blur and a colour split as it lets go. The other looks: BLACK FLASH, black lightning cracking out of the point in a crimson glow over a pale negative; CINEMATIC, a white-out with the rod burned black into it and a shockwave ring split in colour tearing out across the frozen picture), a blinding white-gold burst with god rays fanning out of it, the land rippling out in rings
  like water for a few seconds, a hemispherical shockwave bending the light, a narrow jet of molten rock shot some 300
  up its path and falling back by 6 s, 256 chunks of rock thrown up in a steep cone (two in five of them molten,
  glowing and cooling as they fly), molten spatter landing round it and cooling, a short plume of dust (about 90
  high, spreading wider and wider into a haze as it rises and fades, gone 13 s after) and a low ring of dust racing
  out at 343 blocks a second. The rod drives in and stays, the crater blasted out round it in the next 0.7 s, out from the middle
  with the shock, then lined from the rod out (its blocks in a second after it strikes) over the next 0.8 s, the rod standing in the crater's middle, the floor round its foot heaved up in broken slabs, its blocks glowing in
  slow bands of light while the strike runs, its tip shining, its light rising softly out of the crater and the air
  over it shimmering, the crater's floor white-hot round its foot and cooling over about 20 s, the column of light
  calming into a quiet pillar. The crack-boom arrives late with the distance, the ground's thump first; the camera
  and the picture are jolted hard (sharper and shorter than the Nuke's) and the player knocked back and up (none past
  450). It reaches 1000 blocks. **Photosensitivity:** the impact frames are a fifth of a second of hard black, white and gold cuts over the whole picture;
  `/orbital impactframes off` or OFF on the wheel's IMPACT FX row leaves them out.

- **Supercell** (page three, `/orbital supercell`). A storm called down on the target, no beam. Over six and a half seconds the sky
  darkens and a storm cloud boils up over the target and spreads 300 blocks out: a low dark deck, a wall cloud hanging
  under its middle turning slowly with ragged scud round it, a tower climbing into an anvil that spreads downwind; rain
  falls out of its base in streaked grey curtains drifting with the wind, a few streaks falling past the eye under it
  (the pass draws the rain: the world's weather is left alone). The lightning builds with no pause: from 4 s small,
  thin lone bolts strike the land under it out to 120 blocks, one every second or two and coming faster; from 15 s
  they come in waves, more and more at once, up to five, a wave every half second or less; from 24 s their feet draw
  in on the target and their roots in under the wall cloud's middle, until the last wave strikes round the target at
  about 27.7 s (about 130 bolts in all). Each is drawn by the pass as a branching channel from the cloud's base that
  re-strikes once or twice, the cloud lit from inside round its root and the land round its foot lit bright, with
  flickers in the cloud between. Then the finale's run-in: twenty bolts in a rapid run from 27.93 to 28.81 s, each
  there at once and gone in a few frames, on the cracks of the finale's sound (closing from 0.22 s apart to 0.025),
  landing closer and closer onto the target, some arcing in out of a ring round the wall cloud. Three thin funnels come down out of the wall cloud
  (6.5 to 28 s), touch, wander over the land for five seconds or so in a swirl of dust and lift back up, their roar
  heard where they are. In the hush before the crack (from 28.84 s) the light goes out, the land and the cloud a
  breath darker, only the one channel's leader racing down; at 29 s a single blinding channel strikes the target, the
  ring of bolts round the wall cloud snapping into it in the same instant, held for two seconds and re-struck again and
  again: a whiteout, a shockwave racing out over the land at 140 blocks a second with a
  wall of dust behind it, the crater glowing, a jolt and a shove at the Rod's strength. The ground trembles more and
  more as the bolts draw in. From 32.5 s the rain stops and the storm dies away; it ends at 42 s. It reaches 1000
  blocks.

- **Hurricane** (page three, `/orbital hurricane`). A hurricane spun up over the target, no beam, some 3,500 blocks
  across and 450 high. While the designator is held its countdown reads `TRACKING STORM`. Over the first six seconds
  a ragged mass of towering storms gathers over the target; to about 16 s it is drawn into three trailing rain bands
  wound tighter and tighter round a dense core, each part coming together at its own time, the core first. The eye
  opens from a pinhole into a stadium by about 17 s: a billowed wall flaring out as it rises, its far side sunlit from
  the rim, clear sky straight overhead, an open moat round its foot, and from above one shield of cloud running out
  from the eyewall's top with the bands feathering out of it and cirrus streaming over it all. Rain falls in the world
  round you (the world's weather is left alone), none in the eye. Lightning bursts in the eyewall at 16-28 s and again
  at 43-46 s, about a third of it bolts out of the eyewall's flank across the moat, the brightest flashes lighting the
  cloud; the thunder arrives late with the distance. The wind spins up to full strength by 26 s (40 blocks a second at
  the eyewall). From 15 s its front tears the land up, out from the eyewall (62 blocks out) in to 32 and out to 150 by
  35 s: the plants and leaves stripped first, then the trees, the ground's top blocks and the buildings, roofs first.
  Nothing appears in the air: every block rises out of the land behind the front and climbs round the eyewall in
  thirteen rings, in the game's own textures, coloured by what was torn up under each ring; leaves and glass are seen
  through as the game draws them. Most trees are shredded where they stand; others go up whole, snap at the trunk
  (leaving a stump of one to four logs) or are lifted whole and break apart in the air into twelve tumbling pieces;
  pieces of buildings are lifted off whole too. Mobs and dropped items are carried round with it (terrain on). With
  KNOCKBACK on the wind picks you up when its front reaches you (anywhere from the eye's edge out to 175 blocks,
  flying, standing, falling, gliding or swimming), eases you into the spin and carries you round the eyewall with the
  debris, about 108 blocks out and 50 up, buffeted by gusts, your view turning a little with you (`/orbital carryview
  still` keeps it your own). **To break free, hold sneak for half a second**; a creative player is put back into
  flight. While you fly you are left alone; stop flying (land, or drop out of flight in mid-air) for a second while
  the wind still blows where you are and it grabs you again. At 46 s the winds drop and everything is flung out: the
  field's blocks fall and shatter to dust where they hit, the trees and the pieces settle into the poses they land in
  as real blocks, and you are swept out and thrown at about 47 s, out, round with the wind and up, then left to the
  game's physics (a player who can't fly gets 12 s of Resistance V so the landing doesn't kill them). Everything is
  down by about 52 s; from 54 s the eyewall collapses, the eye fills and the cloud thins away from its edges, all of it
  gone by 63.4 s; it ends at 64 s. It reaches 1000 blocks. **Photosensitivity:** the eyewall's lightning is bright
  flashes over much of the picture, in bursts.

- **Tsunami** (page three, `/orbital tsunami`). No beam: the sea stands up. Over the first three seconds a dark line, a swell, then a wall
  rises across the whole horizon beyond the target, 320 blocks high and over five kilometres wide, about 1,200 blocks from
  you, straight as a rule, a thin torn white lip and spray blowing off its top, and the sky goes a dark grey. It comes in
  at about 100 blocks a second, losing height over the land to some 145 over the target's ground, and starts to tear:
  stretches of its crest pitch forward, long streaks of foam are drawn up its grey-green, almost black face, fine chop
  runs over all of it, its foot turns into a brown boiling bore with what it has swept up in it, mist is pushed along the
  ground ahead of it, its shadow takes the land, and close by, torn fans of spray are thrown up where it hits trees, buildings and rises. Its foot
  is held back by high ground and runs on up the low, so it wraps the hills and tongues up the valleys.
  It reaches the target at 9 seconds and you a few seconds after. The blow is a white flash, the picture thrown round,
  and a second or two of foam thrown past at a violent speed, opening on dark water; then the green water, bright
  overhead and with light coming down through it, silt and bubbles streaming by, wreckage going past (a whole tree, a
  corner of a house, a second tree), lit by the water, dark underneath, going into the murk with distance. The water
  hauls you up and you break the surface 6.5 seconds after it took you, on a brown torrent running back the way it came,
  wreckage floating by, and ride it down as it falls: hard at first, slowing as it nears the land, hilltops first, the
  plains under a few blocks of running water for a while, wet land and rills as it goes. It sets you down near where it
  found you, facing the way it went, the sky clearing as the strike ends.

All of them:

- **Real camera shake.** Booms and impacts shake the actual camera, timed to when the blast reaches you and weaker the
  further away you are.
- **Everything is in the world.** The effects are traced against the depth buffer and against shapes placed in the
  world (planes, cylinders, a volume), so they stay put when you move, sit behind hills in front of them and are seen in
  perspective. Nothing is painted flat on the screen but the lens: bloom, a streak and the flash.
- **Real terrain damage.** The crater, the shaft to bedrock and what each laser leaves behind are real blocks.
- **Point and fire.** Hold the designator on a target for under a second and it fires where your crosshair lands, up to
  200 blocks away (1000 for the Mothership, the Downfall, the Planet Killer, the Crystal Bloom and the Sinkhole).
- **Custom sound design.** Each laser has its own charge-up, lock-on, impact, beam, rumble and spool-down, synthesized
  from scratch, under vanilla explosions, thunder and the sound of the ground breaking.
- **Quiet.** Nothing it does shows up in chat.

## What each strike destroys

Every strike is permanent. Players are spared. The ship lasers and the Planet Killer keep the chunks they work on loaded while they work.


- **Plasma, Godly, Black Hole:** everything above the ground flattened within about 44 blocks (52 for the Black
  Hole), a crater about 60 across, a shaft to bedrock, the land scorched round it, every mob and dropped item within
  40 blocks killed. Players are spared.
- **Meteor Storm:** craters across 120 blocks round the target, then everything above the ground flattened out to
  100, a crater about 125 across, the land charred out to 135, rubble out to 250, every mob within 130 killed.
- **Mothership:** the land vaporised out to 34 blocks, a crater 22 across, a shaft to bedrock, and a trench 100 blocks
  long, up to 15 wide and 17 deep, cut away from you. The ship lasers keep the chunks they work on loaded.
- **Downfall:** a kilometre-long wreck buried at least 360 blocks in front of you, the land under its whole length
  crushed, the ground it shoved aside heaped along both sides, then the black hole's crater with arms out to 150
  blocks and a shaft to bedrock, every mob under the ship and round the hole killed. The crushing goes on for up to a
  minute after the strike, and under the far end of the hull it may be skipped past what the game has loaded.

## The mod

The mod ([`mod/`](../mod)) is Rust built for WebAssembly, running in Horizon's mod sandbox. It is one folder: the code
(`mod.wasm`), the designator with its model and texture, the laser icons, and the sounds. It uses these capabilities:

| Capability | What the mod does with it |
|---|---|
| `input` | claims the designator's right-clicks (use-start, use-stop, ticks held), registers the **R** keybind (it opens the wheel only with the designator in hand), and reads what you type in the wheel's speed box |
| `ui` | draws the laser wheel: quads for the segments, item icons, text; clicks come back as events |
| `render` | the strike's post-processing pass ([`mod/shaders/strike.wgsl`](../mod/shaders/strike.wgsl)), fed 16 numbers each frame, more through `render.set-data` (the Crystal Bloom's spires and pieces, and where each piece comes to rest), and the camera shake |
| `world` | aims (a raycast up to 1024 blocks, across the distant terrain past the loaded chunks), plays the sounds, runs the crater's block changes as server commands, silently, in bulk each tick (the big lasers' land goes straight into the chunks' sections through `world.edit-blocks`), asks the height of the ground under the Crystal Bloom's pieces and stumps (`world.surface-height`), reads the real blocks of the land the Sinkhole cuts into slabs and the Payload and the Nuke sweep and scorch (`world.read-columns`: the names of the blocks round each column's top), sets your flying speed, throws you back as the Nuke's shockwave passes (`world.push-player`), carries you round the Hurricane (`world.carry-player`), and asks whether you are flying (`game.player-flying`) |
| `atlas` | the game's own block textures in the post pass (`atlas.block-sprites`, bindings 8 and 9), so the Hurricane's flying blocks and trees look like the blocks they were |
| `hud`, `storage`, `game-read` | the status panel, the chosen laser and terrain setting, the player's position and dimension |
| `registries` | the items, their textures and models, and the sounds |

The first four are new: they are on Horizon's roadmap ("planned interfaces" in its
[docs/mods.md](https://github.com/Stink-O/horizon/blob/mod-api-input-ui-render-world/docs/mods.md)), and the
[fork](https://github.com/Stink-O/horizon/tree/mod-api-input-ui-render-world) implements them so that mods built for
Horizon's API 0.1 still load unchanged. The mod reads its events with `input.poll` instead of the game calling new hooks,
which is what keeps that true. The fork's later additions (`world.set-flying-speed`, `input.poll-typed`,
`world.edit-blocks`, `world.surface-height` and `render.set-data` from the release `orbital-mod-api-5`,
`world.read-columns` from `orbital-mod-api-6`, and `world.push-player`, the Nuke's knockback, from
`orbital-mod-api-8`, and the `atlas` capability, `world.carry-player` and `game.player-flying`, the Hurricane's, from
`orbital-mod-api-10`) are needed too: a fork build without them won't start the mod, so 0.5.8 needs
`orbital-mod-api-10` or newer. `orbital-mod-api-7` added a half-size stage to a mod's post pass (no new import: the
pass's source says whether it has one), which QUALITY FAST uses; `orbital-mod-api-9` skips a post pass the graphics
driver can't build instead of crashing.

A strike's world changes go through a budget of 20,000 blocks and 400 commands a tick, and the rest wait for the next
tick. The Planet Killer's crater is too big for commands, so it is written straight into the chunks with
`world.edit-blocks`, a run up each column at a time, about 1.5 million blocks a second, without telling the blocks round
it (no sand falls, no water or lava flows).

Each call into the mod has a fuel limit (a tick about 50 million WebAssembly instructions); past it the game stops the
mod ("it took too long"). A work meter (`mod/src/meter.rs`) counts each call's work and puts off what can wait (a
stage of a strike's plan, an edit, a command) to the next tick, and the heavy plans are split into small stages, so no
laser comes near it. Block edits are also paced by the frame time (`mod/src/pace.rs`): each chunk an edit changes is
sent to the client again whole, so while frames run long fewer edits go in a tick, and a big wave's outer rings take a
little longer instead of stalling the picture. The fork's `orbital-mod-api-8` makes those re-sent chunks much cheaper
for the client.

## The effects

The effects are one fragment shader, run as the mod's post pass after the fork's small prelude (the world's colour and depth, the camera and the mod's parameters). It rebuilds every pixel's position from
the depth buffer, so ground effects (the reticle, the sigil, the crater glow, the shockwaves) sit on real blocks. The beams
and pillars are cylinders found by the closest approach between the view ray and their axis, the halo rings and wings are
planes in the world, Plasma's fireball is a noise volume traced along the view ray, and Godly's opening in the clouds is
a plane far overhead. The Black Hole marches each view ray that passes near it through its gravity (the photon orbit
equation), picking up the accretion disk where the ray crosses it and resampling the scene in the direction the ray
leaves in; the debris round it are ray-traced boxes on paths the mod's block changes follow too. The Meteor Storm's meteors are
placed by the same hash in the shader and in the mod, so each crater opens where its meteor lands; their trails, the
fireball and the smoke are noise volumes traced along the view ray. The Mothership's hull is found analytically: each
view ray meets its stepped decks as flat floors and its walls by testing the outline, so it is cheap however much of the
sky it covers; its arrival and departure are averaged over a shutter for real motion blur. The Downfall has a pass of its
own ([`mod/shaders/downfall.wgsl`](../mod/shaders/downfall.wgsl), after the helpers in `strike.wgsl`), so its size costs the
other lasers nothing: the same hull, met in its own frame as it tilts (from any side, roof and batteries too), the
Black Hole's lens, and its dust as a volume integrated along the view ray in closed form. Its fall, crash, gouge,
chunks and the blocks the black hole tears up are worked out by the same formulas in the shader and in the mod. The
Planet Killer has its own pass too ([`mod/shaders/planetkiller.wgsl`](../mod/shaders/planetkiller.wgsl)): its towers are
solids marched in their own frame (a signed distance: plinth, buttresses, tiers, collars, crown; sheared as they lean),
the nearest hiding what is behind it, its bolts are jagged lines from a seeded hash, met kink by kink by the
view ray (their broad bends first, the kinks only near them), so each keeps the path it took from frame to frame, the
ring is met where the view ray crosses its plane (a clean ellipse from any side), its heart, dome and fireballs are
traced as spheres and domes, the towers' dust and the mushroom cloud are noise volumes marched along the view ray, and
the dust wall is a cylinder; its towers and its chasms are placed by the same hash in the shader and in the mod, and the
crater glows where it has just been opened as the mod digs it from the middle out. The Crystal Bloom has its own pass
too ([`mod/shaders/crystalbloom.wgsl`](../mod/shaders/crystalbloom.wgsl)): each spire is a convex crystal (a hexagonal
prism drawing in up its length under a six-faced tip, its faces a little uneven), so the view ray is met exactly, plane
by plane, and the face it enters by is a real facet; inside it the ray is bent in (each colour a little differently),
followed to the face it leaves by and out again, past the cleavage sheets inside, through the crystal's body, and the
nearest spire hides what is behind it. The shards are octahedra met the same way, the glitter is points of light on
shells round the burst found where the view ray crosses each, and the dust is a noise volume. The spires, their breaks,
the pieces' cuts, throws and spins and the fissures are worked out once by the mod as the strike starts (`cb_table`)
and handed to the pass with `render.set-data`, so the pass reads them rather than hashing them again for every pixel
(about half its GPU time at the heaviest moments). Each stump is built where its spire stood, each piece is built as
blocks where it lands, and the crater glows only where the mod has really dug it. The pass only knows the land at the target's height, so the mod asks the world how high the ground
really is under each of the ~200 pieces that fly out (`world.surface-height`, a pair a tick from 13 s) and rests each one
whole on it: on a low point of the ground under it, so it is wedged in rather than hovering, deeper into the high side
of a slope or a cliff where it would otherwise hang over the ground. It gives the pass how far up or down that is
(`render.set-data`), long before the burst; the pass throws the piece that much higher over its flight, so the last pose
it draws is exactly where the blocks are built, block for block the same piece. The stumps are built where they stand,
down to the ground wherever it lies lower, and the crystals round their feet rest on the ground at their feet.

## The sounds

The sounds ([`tools/synth_sounds.py`](../tools/synth_sounds.py)) have no samples in them: they are generated from noise
and sweeps, with a reverb built from a synthetic impulse response. The Planet Killer's bursts
([`tools/sounds_pk_blast.py`](../tools/sounds_pk_blast.py)) are shaped to measurements of a real, very large explosion:
its spectrum, how it rises and holds, how its bands move and how wide it sounds over time. Only the numbers were taken
from the recording; none of its audio is used. The Crystal Bloom's ([`tools/sounds_crystal.py`](../tools/sounds_crystal.py))
are built from the modes of struck glass (inharmonic partials, each a pair of sines a hair apart so it shimmers), glints
of a few milliseconds by the thousand, and bursts of filtered noise for glass breaking; its detonation is the Planet
Killer's `explosion_live` on its own seed and with its own character, with ringing crystal, glitter and shattering glass
on top of it. The Sinkhole's ([`tools/sounds_sinkhole.py`](../tools/sounds_sinkhole.py)) are rock and earth: a sub-bass
rumble that swells and sags unevenly, groans of the ground under strain (narrow bands of noise sliding down, rock
grinding in uneven grabs), sharp cracks of rock each with its own splinters and a crumble of grit, the give's drop, the
collapse (`explosion_live` again, on its own seed, made earthy: its top rolled off, its low-mid pushed, a strain before
it, rock grinding as it gives), the rockfall (a grinding roar and slabs striking the bottom, unevenly, further and
further off) and the pebbles after, each bouncing its own way, in the pit's long echo. Each is heard late by how far
away it is. The Payload's ([`tools/sounds_payload.py`](../tools/sounds_payload.py)): the entry's double crack and sub boom as it strikes the thick air; its crackle, distinct uneven pops, four to seven a second, a few much bigger, heard from kilometres up (their top taken off by the air, each smeared over 25-35 ms, the big ones rumbling cracks, faint dull echoes off the land a few tenths of a second to over a second later); a faint thin whine high up, a far rumble under it, one narrow, slowly moving sound overhead with its top dulled by the air (the torn air's flutter and the dive's rush only opening out round the player in the last second); the dive's whistle bending up, the pops thicker and a roar of air and fire tearing louder, cut
dead at the strike; the ground's thump and rumble arriving first, through the land at five times the air's speed
(`ground`), with a faint early jolt; a near-silent breath while the blast travels; the blast (`explosion_live` on its
own seed, as loud as the Planet Killer's; from 500 to 1,500 blocks off cross-faded into the same blast through that much
air, its top 10 dB down, `blast_far`); the dust's rush and the wind gusting on after it; clods thudding down round the player and a
patter of grit; and a long tail of rolls off the land, further and further off. `/orbital payloadsound alt` swaps the blast and the
thump for alternatives in the manner of a sound designer's redesign (`blast_alt`, `ground_alt`, `blast_alt_far`: the low boom first, round 50 Hz and broad, sliding down into a second blow, a bright crack, then a wide roar over a heaving 35-65 Hz weight, with the lows' own harmonics so it carries on small speakers too); `main`
swaps them back; the wheel's BLAST SOUND row (shown while the Payload is loaded or pointed at) does the same.
The Nuke's ([`tools/sounds_nuke.py`](../tools/sounds_nuke.py)): its fall, one dark roar and a falling whistle high
overhead, cut dead on the burst; the flash is silent; the ground's thump and shudder first, through the land; the blast
(`explosion_live` stretched long, a second crack off the ground 50-70 ms after the first, a boom sweeping from ~46 Hz
down to ~31, a heaving sub tail, the lows' own harmonics for small speakers, the boom coming back off far land and the
fireball's crackle in distant clusters), cross-faded from 300 to 900 blocks off into the same blast through that much
air (`blast_far`, its mids kept so it still carries on a laptop); the shockwave's gust and grit going past, a third as
loud past about 1,100 blocks; debris thudding and pattering down within 700 blocks; and a long rumble rolling in off
the land for half a minute. `/orbital nukesound alt` (or the BLAST SOUND row) swaps the blast for the Payload's ALT scaled up
(`blast_alt`, `blast_alt_far`): a tonal boom dropping from ~68 Hz to ~40 with its harmonics stacked on it, a sharp,
heavy crack, two more blows after it, then a long thunder roll and the boom echoing off far land.
The Rod's ([`tools/sounds_rod.py`](../tools/sounds_rod.py), on the Nuke's tools): its fall is heavenly rather than
fiery, a sung chord (a stack of fifths on F#3, `oo` opening to `ah`) out of the sky, climbing a fourth and swelling as it
nears, glassy partials over it and a bowed-metal resonance under it, a rush of air sliding up in the last second, one
source high overhead; a hypersonic crack a tenth of a second before the impact and the chord cut dead on it. The
ground's thump comes first (~46 Hz falling to ~22, a shudder); then the blast: one very hard crack and at once a tonal
boom (~60 Hz dropping to ~45 and on to ~40, its octave, twelfth and driven harmonics stacked on it), a sub push, the
ground's rebound 0.24 s on, a clang (a giant metal spike struck end-on, its low modes ringing on for a second or so)
and a crunch of rock, then a short dry tail (a few seconds of thunder roll, four echoes off far land); cross-faded
from 250 to 800 blocks off into the same blast through that much air (`blast_far`); the rumble rolling in for ~20 s;
debris landing within 500 blocks; and the rod left standing hums as it cools (a low resonant hum on B1, the clang's
modes singing faintly, the metal ticking and groaning, ~26 s, placed at the rod). Every layering of them peaks under
-1 dBTP.

The Supercell's ([`tools/sounds_storm.py`](../tools/sounds_storm.py)) model the thunder rather than draw it: each
bolt's channel is a tortuous path from the ground up into the cloud and on along its base, with branches, and every
few metres of it sends its own short pressure pulse (an N-wave, longer and duller the further it has come), arriving
as far behind the flash as that bit of the channel is far away and loudest where the channel crosses the line of sight;
three to five return strokes 40-90 ms apart re-fire it. So a close bolt (`crack`, 6 takes, within 90 blocks) rips
and cracks bright, then booms (a falling 46-58 Hz fundamental with its harmonics stacked on it, so a phone hears the
weight, its body gently overdriven) and rolls in off the rest of the channel and the land's echoes, over six seconds;
90-260 blocks off (`crack_mid`, 5 takes) it is a duller clap with a little tearing left and a longer roll; further
(`crack_far`, 5 takes) it is mostly rumble, the clap a soft knock under a deep, long roll; a flicker in the cloud
(`rumble`, 5 takes, none in the seconds round the finale nor within 0.4 s of a bolt's) is a long, soft roll high up,
narrow and dark, under the bolts. Each is late by the distance (343 blocks a second), 4 dB quieter each time the
distance doubles, the flurry's small bolts 5.5 dB quieter than the waves', never starting within 0.15 s of another
(it would flam: bolts heard closer together are one clap, their power added, so the waves' thunder comes thicker and
louder as they grow), let down a little (never more than 4.4 dB) while louder thunder still rolls, and a take of its own: the plan picks it (each take is its own event too) and never
one of the last two of its kind, its pitch spread 0.9-1.06. The storm builds out of a hush (the wind rising in gusts, a low
pressure swell, leaves stirring, far rolls, the first heavy drops); the wind (a buffet, a rush, a howl climbing with
each gust, leaves thrashing) and the rain (a wide wash of drops, near drops ticking and plipping, heavier ones on the
ground, a low drumming; the only rain there is, the pass drawing it) overlap through the storm. The finale is a detonation with lightning in it. It
starts 1.2 s before its crack with the last waves' cracks stacking up faster and louder and the air fizzing; 0.26 s
before the crack the plan cuts the storm's beds (`stopsound`: the rain, the wind, the funnels' roar, every bolt's and
flicker's thunder still rolling) under its last, loudest cracks, and 0.16 s before it all of it falls away into a hush,
only the near channels' tearing and the air drawn in rising into it; then everything lands at once, as hard as the Rod's
blast (its loudest instant louder): eight channels' crack-blast re-struck by the return strokes, a driven tonal boom
(62 Hz falling to 31) on a sub push, a thump you feel, a 40-250 Hz body, a dark boiling roar with rock crushed in it, a
buzzing arc and the discharge crackling; then the strike's own thunder rolling off round the sky for fifteen seconds,
the boom's echoes off the land, debris raining down for six seconds, and from 0.9 s after the crack the rain and a
little wind coming back under it (`after`). Cross-faded from 250 to 800 blocks off into the same strike through that
much air (`finale_far`), as the Rod's blast. With the crack comes a jolt as hard as the Nuke's, the Rod's push, and the
picture slammed (as the Rod's) and punched pale (`sc_press`; drawn in, its edges darkening, through the hush). Each funnel's
roar (mono, a deep rumble, the freight-train roar boiling over it, debris rattling in it) is played where the funnel is
every second, three-second copies whose constant-power windows overlap into one steady roar that follows it.
`python3 tools/sounds_storm.py --listen DIR` writes the strike's mix as heard 60, 150, 500 and 1500 blocks off, mixed
as Horizon plays it (every file summed to mono, as the game decodes it, 0.596 in each ear, on its tick, then the game's
output limiter): every file's decoded true peak is under -1.15 dBTP.

The Hurricane's ([`tools/sounds_hurricane.py`](../tools/sounds_hurricane.py)) are three continuous beds a minute long,
each played once from the strike's start with the storm's whole life written into it, so nothing restarts or steps:
the wind round you (a hush, then gusts rising out of it, buffeting, a howl climbing with each gust, as hard as the
storm blows at that moment), the eyewall's roar (a deep turbulent rumble with a freight-train roar boiling over it;
`roar_far`, darker, past 330 blocks) and wind-driven rain; the plan only sets their levels by the distance. Over them,
now and then and quieter, debris in the gusts, trees cracking and tearing out where they are torn up, and whooshes
going past while you are carried (none in the eye), built from field-recording pieces (`tools/hu_foley.py`). The
eyewall's lightning has its thunder, timed to the pass's flashes and late by the distance: a bright ragged crack
within about 110 blocks, a duller clap with a longer roll to 320, mostly roll further off, and a roll up in the cloud
for a flash that strikes nothing. `--listen DIR` mixes it as the game plays it at 60, 150 and 500 blocks and carried.

The Tsunami's ([`tools/sounds_tsunami.py`](../tools/sounds_tsunami.py)) are 23 mono files, 2 MB, all synthesised, with no resonators in the water at all: thirty third-octave bands of independent noise, each with its own chaotic gain in decibels (slow eddies, heavy-tailed regional surges, splashes), broadband blows in which every band rises at once, and debris as noise bursts buried in the roar (Horizon
decodes every file to mono and plays a global sound dead centre, so nothing is built stereo). A rupture and a long rumble
from the first second; a roar in three layers that overlap into one rising wall, `roar_far` from 2.5 s, `roar_mid` 9 s
and `roar_near` 4 s before the water reaches the player (its last second is the immersion cut into it: the level drops
9 dB in 3 ms and the top closes in a third of a second); the crash at the target, late by the distance (`crash`, and
`crash_far` crossfaded in from 250 to 800 blocks: a heave, a slam of water, hundreds of trunks and beams, masonry); the
`engulf` slam as the water goes over the head, the muffled churning bed `under`, `surface` at 6.5 s, the `torrent` ridden
down, the `drain` and its far roar `roar_back`, `rumble_after`, and a dry-up `trickle`; `roar_pass` for a player the wave
never closes over; and short positional trunk cracks and
water slaps placed where the front is as it reaches the ground round the player (`tools/sounds_tsunami.py`'s `schedule`
and `strike/tsunami.rs` use the same formulas: the arrival `tp = 9 + dp / 100 + 1.07e-5 dp^2`, the sound's lateness
`dp / 343`). The strike's mix stays under -1 dBTP at every distance from 100 to 720 blocks (the script's `--listen DIR`
writes it as heard from 200 and 600 blocks, mixed as the game plays it, with spectrograms).

The Sinkhole's pass ([`mod/shaders/sinkhole.wgsl`](../mod/shaders/sinkhole.wgsl)) reads the rings and slabs the mod
cuts the land into (`sh_table`, through `render.set-data` on the tick after it fires), each slab's ground height and
slope once the world has said them (`world.surface-height`), the waterfalls, where the rim's columns stand under
water, and the land itself. As it fires the mod reads the 24 blocks under the top of every column of the pit
(`world.read-columns`, six queries of ~8,000 columns) and works them out ~3,000 columns a tick
([`mod/src/strike/sh_land.rs`](../mod/src/strike/sh_land.rs)) into three words a column: its ground's height, the block
on top, the one under it and one deeper (by name: about 60 materials, each a top and side colour from the game's own
textures and how much it varies block to block; grass and most leaves tinted by the biome's grass and foliage colours
the world answers with, the poplars', cherry's, azalea's, pale oak's, spruce's and birch's leaves their own colours),
snow lying on it, a trunk and the leaves over it, the slab it is part of, and whether water stands on it (a slab
carries the bed, and no crack crosses water); plus each slab's bounds. Columns not loaded yet are asked again, and a
slab is drawn as its land only once all of it is in.
The pass draws each slab as those blocks, marching the view ray through its columns cell by cell (a 3D DDA inside its
bounds, after a bounding sphere), each face shaded as the game shades it and textured with texels that fade out once
smaller than a pixel; it draws it in its place from the moment the mod digs the land under it (the same blocks where
the real ones were), then tips and drops it, its trees going with it, lit as the land round it (the sky's light for the time of day) until
it sinks below where its ground was, then into the pit's shadow and dark by how deep it is. A slab whose land was not read is a block of
grass over dirt over stone, as before. The dig clears each column up to that real top. The cracks are the slabs' own
edges, reached by spurts racing out from the middle (`sh_spurts` in the mod, `sh_crack_at` in the pass), drawn only on
the ground's top (the land's heights), their width against the pixel's footprint across them from the derivatives of
the ground's position, never thinner on screen than most of a pixel (wider and fainter instead) and faded out far off,
the column under a pixel looked up a little inside the block it is on, so nothing flickers; the waterfalls pour from
the water's own surface over the rim as it wanders, over a lip and faster and faster a little out from the wall, in
strands of the water's biome tint with darker gaps, white where they break up, mist into the dark;
the pit is darkened where it has really been dug, the dark fading out over the strike's last 9 s; the dust is a noise
volume marched along the view ray.


The Payload's pass ([`mod/shaders/payload.wgsl`](../mod/shaders/payload.wgsl)) reads the object's path, sampled 128
times from 3 to 13.6 s, and the column's eighteen billows (`pl_table`, through `render.set-data` on the tick after it
fires; the player's position as it fired is in its params), and the pieces it sheds (from `PL_FR`: when each lets go,
where and how fast, how long it burns; each then flies with drag and gravity worked out in the pass), and the 120
debris chunks' numbers (from `PL_DB`, `pl_debris_words`: worked out once there rather than for every chunk at every
pixel; each pixel looks for the land under a chunk only once it is near its ray). The trail is drawn
through the path's samples as soft-edged smoke: each piece is the slab between two planes across the trail (through
its ends, halfway round the bend at each, so the pieces fit with no seam at any angle) and the ray's whole way through
it is counted (the profile along the ray integrated with erf, longer and denser seen glancing along it). Three octaves of
noise carried with it push its edge and density in and out, looked up on the sheet through its axis that faces the eye;
each lets go towards its mean (and the bead threshold to what it would let through on the whole) where a pixel spans
several of them or where the ray runs along the trail through several, so it neither shimmers far off nor streaks
end-on. It is carried by the wind, swelling as it ages and breaking into beads as its threshold rises; eight samples at
a time are first tested as one ball, so most rays skip it; the column is marched through the billows' smooth union (worked out once a pixel), its surface warped and bulged in
cellular domes at three scales (36, 14 and 5.5 blocks, each dome its own size and each scale's lattice turned its own
way, so no grid shows; the big ones fewer up its stem) and frayed at its edge by fine
noise, its density a ramp whose width varies over it (crisp to wispy), stepped finely as the ray closes on its edge (and in long strides through the air round it, the stride growing on a
ray that skims it), the billows kept as half floats from the target so the march keeps its registers, a cheap bound
round the whole first so most of the picture never works out where the billows are,
each lump lit by a look a few blocks towards the sun and the column's bulk shadowing it with three more (looked for
afresh while what lies behind still shows through, kept deeper in), lit by the sky the scene shows; as it settles its lumps sink back, its edge
becomes a broad soft fringe mottled by noise and its density thins away; the dust is marched only through the
shell just behind the shockwave's front (and sparsely inside it), over the land's own ground: a 96 x 96 heightfield
of it asked of the world as it fires (`world.surface-height`, from about 260 to 1,150 blocks round the target as the
player is near or far), sent from `PL_HF` with how sheltered each cell is from the blast by the land between, and close
to where each pixel's ray meets the land, that point's own height, so the dust climbs the rises, fills the hollows and
is thinner and later in the lee of a ridge; it stops at the column. As the front washes over the eye (within about 40
blocks of the ground), the picture is greyed and flattened by the dust, clearing over a few seconds, and over the second
after it muck lands on the lens, out of focus, as measured from a reference clip's dirt: drops near round but each its
own (a few stretched), 2 to 7% of the picture's height, a ring darker than the middle, soft at the edge, of very
different strengths, often with a smaller drop beside them, nearly all high on the screen, fading over ten seconds or so; the wall is thinned right round the eye so the picture over it stays clear; the debris is 120 ballistic chunks with drag, torn up across the blast's middle, each blurred
along its motion, tumbling, the big ones trailing smoke. It costs about 0.3 to 2.5 ms at the preview's 800x450 (2.8 ms
from 230 blocks with the column filling the view), and 10 to 11 ms at 1920x1080 with the column large.

The Nuke's pass ([`mod/shaders/nuke.wgsl`](../mod/shaders/nuke.wgsl)) is the Payload's grown up and shares its layout
(the path from `PL_P`, the heightfield from `PL_HF`, 160 debris chunks from `PL_DB`, `nk_table` in
[`strike/nuke.rs`](../mod/src/strike/nuke.rs)). Its mushroom cloud is one raymarched body: a torus for the cap's roll,
an ellipsoid crown over it, the stem and the base surge's roll and sheet, all smoothly joined, then bulged in
cauliflower lumps (capped at `PL_DM`, which the march's bounds, gates and long steps allow for), the lumps turned round
the roll's core as it churns and carried up the stem; lit by the sun through its own bulk and from inside by the
fireball's heat, white to orange to a deep red as it cools. The flash, the shockwave's shell, the cloud's shadow on
the land, the dust and the debris are drawn as the Payload's are. QUALITY FAST marches the cloud, the dust and the trail
at half size. At 1920x1080 FAST costs about 2 to 3.7 ms and FULL 4 to 10.5 ms, the most close in with the cloud filling
the view.

The Rod's pass ([`mod/shaders/rod.wgsl`](../mod/shaders/rod.wgsl)) is the Nuke's made kinetic and holy, on its
layout (the path from `PL_P`, the heightfield from `PL_HF`, 256 debris chunks from `PL_DB`, `rd_table` in
[`strike/rod.rs`](../mod/src/strike/rod.rs)). The rod is a real object: an analytic rod (`rd_rod_sdf`, sphere-traced
near it only, `rd_rod_hit`: 13 thick, 171 long, its bands as rod.rs lays the standing one's blocks, `rd_band`), dark
metal lit by the sun and the sky, its nose white-hot; its light is a halo round it, a glare (`rd_glare`: bloom, an
eight-spiked starburst, a streak, three ghosts strung through the middle of the picture, where `rd_seen` finds it in
sight) and screen-space god rays (`rd_srays`: streaks out from its place on the screen, eight looks at the depth along
the way to it, broken by anything nearer than it). The column of light and the line in it go by how long since the rod
passed each height (`rd_time_at`, searching the path's samples), so they age smoothly with no step. The impact adds
the ripple (`rd_ripple`: the picture drawn from where the heaving land would put it, its rings lit by their slope),
the jet (`rd_jet`, marched in a vertical cylinder: parcels thrown up at all speeds and falling back, looked up by
their speed so their streaks fly with them), the plume (the Nuke's raymarched body, `pl_column`, small, thin and
spreading wide), the molten spatter on the land and the white-hot floor round the rod's foot, and over the standing
rod's own blocks its glow. The impact frames (`rd_impact`; `rd_clock` holds the pass's clock on the hit, `rd_if_cut` counts the cuts in 60 fps frames) threshold the scene's luminance and depth into silhouettes, set the rod in them, and draw thick converging lines from the impact point; each cut is its own composition (`RD_IF_STYLE`: 1 Kanada, the default; 0 Black Flash; 2 cinematic), and `pl_jolt` punches the camera in and shakes it per cut, then smears the release with a zoom blur and a colour split. At
1920x1080 FAST costs about 0.5 ms through the fall, 2.6 to 3.2 ms after the impact from 400 blocks and 3.8 to 4.9 from
150 (inside the plume), FULL 7 to 16 ms.

The Supercell's pass ([`mod/shaders/supercell.wgsl`](../mod/shaders/supercell.wgsl)) reads the bolts, the funnels'

funnels (the vortex section, `vx_*`) are tapered, curving vortices wound with spiral bands, a turning dust cloud at the

along the cloud's base with the flickers; all hidden by what stands in front. QUALITY FAST marches the cloud, the rain and the funnels at half size: at 1920x1080 FAST costs
about 1.8 to 2.8 ms and FULL 2.3 to 8.3 ms (the most under the cloud near the end of the storm).

The Hurricane's pass ([`mod/shaders/hurricane.wgsl`](../mod/shaders/hurricane.wgsl)) reads its table from
[`strike/hurricane.rs`](../mod/src/strike/hurricane.rs) (`render.set-data`): the trees and pieces of buildings it
lifts whole (up to 128, each a rigid group of voxels with its path and, once worked out, where and how it lands), each
ring's colours (a weighted sample of what was torn up under it) and the land's height round the middle. The cloud is
one raymarched volume: its plan (the bands, the dense overcast, the eye and its moat, the cirrus) from gradient noise,
its surfaces displaced by 3D noise rather than its body thinned, lit from its top down with one self-shadow sample, the
march jittered per pixel; it gathers and breaks up soft-edged and is solid at its height. The flying blocks sit in
thirteen rings of 3-block cells round the eyewall, each column of a ring's cells climbing at its own speed so they
never line up into a lattice; blocks, trees and pieces are drawn with the game's own textures (the `atlas` capability:
`atlas.block-sprites`, repacked when the game's resources change), cut-out and translucent faces seen through as the
game draws them, with each block type's average colour as the fallback and for the far land. The rain is lines of
drops in the world round the camera, strung the way they fall and blown with the wind, on grids in four layers out. The
lightning is a fifth-of-a-second slot each in bursts, the brightest flash lighting the cloud. QUALITY FAST marches the
cloud at half size and finds each two by two's nearest block or tree there, the join drawing it sharp.

The Tsunami's pass ([`mod/shaders/tsunami.wgsl`](../mod/shaders/tsunami.wgsl)) is a closed-form wave: the front reaches a
point (s along the wave's way from the target, u across it) at `ts_arr`, the water over it climbs a concave face to its
crest (`ts_prof`, about 105 blocks) and sits a little under that, then all the land's water falls together (`ts_x`,
`ts_fall`: hard, then slowly past the land), its height by `ts_env` (stood up by `ts_rise` over the first three seconds,
320 over the target's ground far out, about 145 at the land, spent at its end, out to 3,300 blocks across), the crest leaning out where it is
breaking (`ts_break`) once it comes in; so nothing is kept from frame to frame, a strike started part way in lands where
it should, and `strike/tsunami.rs` (`TsGeo`) and `surge.rs` use the same functions, in sines rather than noise where they
are asked for hundreds of times a pixel. A ray is marched to the surface and bisected, with long strides over the level
water behind the front. The face is dark grey-green going to black, with a grey sky in it, chop over it, sun glitter, a
little green where it thins, and foam only in its torn lip and in long streaks sheared across the face and drawn up it
(white in curtains, or anywhere near this much of it, reads as a waterfall or a snowy mountain, not as water). Close to,
every edge is cut by `ts_chop`, wavelets from 17 blocks long down to a few pixels whatever the distance, and streaks become a
lace of cell walls (`ts_lace`): nothing there is stretched noise, which is what smears as it fills the frame. The foot
follows the land's height from the table (`ts_ground`), and is held back by it (`ts_toe`, the pass only, up to 0.42 s on
high ground and 0.55 s ahead on low, none round the player, whose engulfing the sounds and the carry have to the moment,
and little enough that the land's edits, which go by the plain arrival, stay under water): round lumps packed together (`ts_boil`), nearly black in their
creases and cream on top, a flat mud grey at a distance. Spray and mist are marched in the band the front lies in; the
rooster tails (`ts_tails`) are drawn on planes across its way at the rows of land just reached, close to the camera only,
ahead of the front only. Wreckage is boxes in cells of a grid in the wave's own frame, in the game's block textures
(`atlas`), kinds chosen from the land's map of trees and buildings. Under the water (camera below the surface) the
picture is rotated and the ray followed to where it leaves the water; twelve steps through foam streaming past (dense at
first, gone in two seconds), silt and bubbles, light shafts, wreckage riding the water's drift and a tree, a house's corner
and a second tree on the sounds' moments (`ts_bigs`), all lit by the water from above, not by the sky. The land the
water has left is soaked dark on the blocks' own pixels, with a dull sheen, pools on the level ground that hold the sky and
what stands round them (a short march through the picture) and dark runs of water down the slopes, drying in patches with
the pools last, the lakes left alone (`ts_wet`, normals from the depth), and the draining surface is
its own shading (`ts_torrent`). At 1920x1080 the pass takes about 5 ms looking at the wave and 2.5 ms under the water.

At QUALITY FAST (the default) the pass is drawn in two stages. `fs_half`, at half
the width and height, draws what is soft and costly (the column, the trail and its glow, the pieces' smoke, the dust)
as premultiplied colour and transmittance, composited in the same order as the whole pass, for each two by two of the
picture's pixels marched to alternately the nearest and the farthest of their depths; beside it go the depth it
marched to, the column's distance and whether any debris still in the air passes near. `fs_join`, at full size, takes
the four nearest of its pixels weighted bilinearly and by how close their depth is to its own pixel's (from the 4 x 4
round it, the nearest in depth, if none is close), so the smoke's edges against blocks and the sky stay where they are,
and then draws what must be sharp: the flash, the falling object, the pieces' glow (hidden behind the column), the
debris (only near where the half stage found some) and the dust at the eye and on the lens. At 1920x1080 with the column
large that is about 3 ms for the half stage and 1 for the join, against 11 for FULL; it looks the same but for the
column's very edge, a touch softer, and the dither in thin dust, a little coarser. The jolt as the front reaches the player is
`render.shake` worked to match the clips: a hard hit, sharp aftershocks every other tick for 0.7 s, then a shudder of
smaller quick jolts fading over the next 1.7 s, over a long faint rumble.
`tools/preview.py payload ... --views plref` looks at it as the clip it follows was filmed (450 blocks off in a wood,
a 59 degree field of view); `plhill`, `plhilllow` and `plhillside` over hills; `pl3far`, `pl3close` and `pl3col` as the
third clip has it (1,600 blocks off looking up at the trail, and 230 off at the object and the column).
## Pausing and leaving the world

A strike plays on the game's ticks, and the game doesn't tick while it is paused, so the pause menu holds the strike
where it is: its clock, the land it is changing and its effects stand still, and the game pauses its sounds and
resumes them where they were. Leaving the world mid-strike ends the strike there: nothing of it goes on into the next
world you open. The chunks it forceloaded are let go the next time you play that world (about two seconds in), so a
save isn't left holding them, even if the game was closed mid-strike. The mod has no hook for leaving a world, so it
finds out on the first tick it is out of one (no player) or in another (the world clock somewhere else).

Horizon itself doesn't stop sounds that are playing when you Save and Quit to Title, so the strike's sounds (and any of
the game's own, a music disc say) can go on over the title screen until they end. That needs a fix in the game.

## Stock Horizon

0.1's setup ran on stock Horizon builds from before 24 October 2026, when Horizon removed shader packs (it won't work on newer ones), with Plasma and Godly (the other lasers are mod-only): a shader pack, a sound pack and the item mod, run by
[`strike.py`](../strike.py) from outside the game through Horizon's `horizon-ctl`. The items are spyglasses there, the remote
opens a chest-style menu to pick the laser, and every strike rewrites the shader pack's settings.

```sh
python3 install.py --legacy --enable    # with the game closed
horizon-ctl launch --world MyWorld --creative --wait
python3 strike.py --doctor              # says what's missing and how to fix it
python3 strike.py --daemon              # hold the designator (slot 9) to fire; the remote (slot 8) picks the laser
```
